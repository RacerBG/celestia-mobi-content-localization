On Wednesday, 18 November 2026 (09:16:07 UTC), NASA's Voyager 1 probe achieved a milestone that no spacecraft has ever done before, reach a distance of one light-day from Earth!

A light-day is equal to a distance of 25.592 billion km (15.902 billion mi) or about 171.07 AU. Launched in 5 September 1977, Voyager 1 embarked on a journey to explore Jupiter, Saturn and their moons before heading out into interstellar space, which it reached in 2012. The venerable probe remains the most distant artificial object from Earth.

Both it and its twin, Voyager 2, are expected to remain operational until at least the 2030s, when their radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) are no longer providing enough electricity to power up any of its instruments and its high gain antenna for communications. It will continue to venture out further into space long after it eventually becomes inactive.
